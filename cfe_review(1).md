# CFE content and toolkit review: rbia-frm-mastermind (v2.2.0)

Reviewer stance: senior CFE. Files read: SKILL.md 8A (l.194-219), references 09/10/11, agents/cfe-investigator.md, scripts/fraud_analytics.py. `/home/claude/pilot-key/` not read. No plugin files modified. Tool runs: `python3 -I -B`, pilot CSVs in `/home/claude/pilot/data`, outputs in `/home/claude/pilot/rerun/cfe/` (edge-case fixtures in `.../cfe/edge/`).
Short verdict: the governance posture (hypotheses not findings, human-only red lines, lead-not-conclusion) is sound and above average. The weaknesses are (a) a tool that silently mishandles real-world data in ways that produce false negatives, (b) an MO library that maps to the ACFE tree only partially and over-claims tool coverage, and (c) thin treatment of the legal/recovery side.

## 2. Overconfident or unsafe statements
- 10:83 evidence hierarchy is presented as fixed. It is a weighting heuristic; admissibility and weight depend on jurisdiction and on authenticity (system logs can be altered by admins). State that.
- 10:75 "Do not alert the subject" with no counterweight on lawful basis for reviewing employee mailboxes/devices (policy notice, proportionality, PDPA 2010 / DPDP Act 2023 / LFPDPPP: VERIFY). 10:93 covers only personal accounts.
- No tipping-off / suspicious-transaction reporting caution (AML duties may bar telling the subject or the counterparty and may make some "negotiated repayment" routes unlawful). No mention of statutory-auditor fraud-reporting triggers (e.g. India Companies Act s.143(12)) that can override "management decides".
- 10:104 says employment and police/regulator decisions are for management, HR and counsel, but omits the case where management/CFO is implicated: no instruction to route via Audit Committee chair and to wall off the implicated executive from the case file. This is a standard CFE independence control.
- 11:153 "rank by materiality (user's thresholds)" is right, but the tool hard-codes unrequested defaults (`recon --tol-pct 10`, `je --round-base 10000`, `duplicates --days 7`, `splits --days 7`, `je` weekend = Sat/Sun). These conflict with the user's rule never to default materiality (config/controls.json `never_default_or_carry_over`). A 10% recon tolerance can hide a systematic 9% over-posting on every line.
- 09:62 and 09:60 are fine as warnings, but presenting prompt-injection and AI-generated documents as ACFE-style "schemes" sits oddly beside a classification the file says follows ACFE; label them "emerging (non-ACFE)".
- Positive: "LEAD" banner, non-additive amount warnings, "no authentication opinion", "no credibility assessment" are correct and well placed.

## 3. Top 12 gaps versus professional CFE practice

1. **Fraud theory / hypothesis cycle**: no ACFE "fraud theory approach" (assume, hypothesise, test, refine, amend) with a stop/continue rule. Hypotheses exist but are not re-ranked as evidence arrives (no Bayesian-style or ACH matrix of evidence vs hypotheses).
2. **Perpetrator/behavioural indicators**: no fraud triangle/diamond, no behavioural red flags (living beyond means, unusual vendor closeness, refusal of leave/rotation, control over records), no Report-to-the-Nations-style context (detection by tip, perpetrator tenure/role, collusion effect). Library is data footprint only.
3. **Intake and case management**: no whistleblower triage form, hotline handling, case-numbering, allegation log, conflict-of-interest check of investigators, or investigation closure criteria.
4. **Financial-profile techniques**: no net-worth, expenditure and bank-deposit methods; no link analysis or timeline construction; no flow-of-funds tracing template (these are core CFE analysis tools, correctly human-gated for personal data, but a plan template is missing).
5. **Digital evidence process**: no preservation priority list with retention clocks (CCTV overwrite, mailbox auto-delete, chat apps, ERP log retention), no forensic-image standard (write-block, SHA-256, time-zone and clock-sync notes), no eDiscovery/keyword-review protocol or privilege screening.
6. **Procurement-fraud lifecycle**: pre-award (spec tailoring, sole-source justification, vendor onboarding), post-award (change-order inflation, product substitution, defective pricing, retrospective POs, ghost deliveries), with matching tests (three-way match, invoice date after payment, vendor paid before creation, dormant vendor reactivation, approver above authority).
7. **Revenue/receivables and inventory analytics**: credit-note and refund abuse, customer kickbacks, lapping detection, aged-receivable write-off patterns, count-sheet and shrinkage analytics. Tool has only `recon` for these.
8. **Payroll analytics**: headcount-to-payroll reconciliation, ghost test (no leave, no attendance, shared bank account), OT versus output capacity, statutory-deduction anomalies. Library lists them but nothing computes them.
9. **Statistical depth**: no outlier/z-score or robust MAD test, no Benford first-two/last-two/second-digit, no number-duplication test, no ratio analysis (max/second-max), no vendor concentration or trend/seasonality baseline.
10. **Legal/regulatory map per jurisdiction**: MKTron operates in India, Malaysia, Mexico. No per-jurisdiction note on mandatory fraud reporting, whistleblower statutes, limitation, privilege (in-house counsel privilege differs), labour-law limits on suspension/deduction, data-protection basis. Currently a blanket `VERIFY JURISDICTION`.
11. **Expert and testimony readiness**: nothing on working-paper standards for litigation (contemporaneous notes, reperformance by a second analyst, report-reviewer independence), nor on engaging a forensic accountant/document examiner (scope letter, instructions, chain of custody hand-off).
12. **Loss quantification methods**: only a principle list. No templates for overbilling (price x quantity delta), phantom scrap (posted minus weighed x market rate), duplicate-payment recovery net of credit notes, range/confidence, interest and investigation cost, or an overlap matrix to avoid double counting.

## 4. Bugs and edge cases in fraud_analytics.py

Pilot run summary (all valid, reproduced): duplicates found SKH-INV-202511 paid twice (rows 9, 13, 373,583); `splits` also flags that same duplicate pair (rows 10, 13) as a "split", so one transaction appears in two tests (non-additive warning applies); `overlap --unmatched` flagged XXXX9999 (first row only); `sod` flagged AP02 (bank change then release, 1.26 days); `recon` at the default 10% flagged only P-1003 (6 months, net 65,107) while tol 0 flags all 27 keys (net 65,713); JE 3 of 4 entries have 4 flags; ratevar net 235,886 (7 invoices at 41.50 v 38.00). Benford correctly NOT_APPLICABLE (n=27, 2 leading digits). `sequence` on the pilot produces a false gap (below).
